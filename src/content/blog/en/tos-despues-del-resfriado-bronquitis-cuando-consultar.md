# A Cough That Lingers After a Cold: Bronchitis and When to Get Checked

Your nose has stopped running, the fever broke days ago and you are back at work. Yet the cough hangs on: it wakes you up at 3 a.m., cuts you off mid-sentence on the phone and gets worse on the stairs. Each fall it is one of the most common reasons people walk into a clinic in southwest Houston, and the question is nearly always the same: has this turned into something else?

Often it has not. Sometimes it has, and the clues are in how long the cough lasts, how it changes and what comes along with it.

## Why the cough outlives the cold

Your body clears a cold virus within days, but it leaves the lining of the bronchial tubes, the airways that carry air into the lungs, raw and irritated. That inflamed lining overreacts to almost anything: a change in temperature, air conditioning, cooking smoke or even a good laugh can set off a coughing fit. That is why the cough is the last symptom to leave.

When the inflammation sits mainly in those airways, it is called **acute bronchitis**, often known as a chest cold. According to the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/acute-bronchitis/about/index.html), the cough from acute bronchitis can last up to about three weeks. It is usually viral, so in most cases the body clears it without an antibiotic; care focuses on easing the cough and watching for signs of something more.

One point that trips people up: yellow or green mucus does not, on its own, mean a bacterial infection. The color comes from your own immune cells at work.

## A week-by-week timeline for your cough

This table is no substitute for an exam, but it can help you see where you stand:

| Time since the cold started | What is usually expected | What makes sense to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week one | Cough with mucus, scratchy throat, fatigue | Rest, fluids and keep an eye on fever |
| Weeks two and three | Cough slowly easing, sometimes dry | Keep up home care; get checked if it is getting worse instead of better |
| Beyond three weeks | No longer typical for a cold | Get checked to find the cause |
| Beyond eight weeks (adults) | Considered a chronic cough | Full evaluation, without putting it off |

The number of days matters, but so does the direction. A cough that is a little gentler each week is on track. A cough that stalls, or improves and then comes roaring back, deserves to have someone listen to your lungs.

## When bronchitis is not the answer

Several conditions pose as "bronchitis that won't clear up." These are the usual suspects:

- **Postnasal drip or sinusitis.** Mucus drains down the back of the throat, especially when you lie down, causing throat clearing and a nighttime cough. Congestion, facial pressure or a bad taste on waking often come with it.
- **Allergies.** A dry cough with bouts of sneezing and itchy eyes that shows up at the same time every year may have nothing to do with your cold. It is evaluated through the [allergy](/services/alergias) service.
- **Asthma or airways left "twitchy."** After a virus, some people notice wheezing, chest tightness, or coughing with exercise and cold air for weeks. People who already have asthma can have a flare triggered by the cold.
- **Some blood pressure medicines.** Certain drugs for hypertension can cause a persistent dry cough. If you started one in recent months, mention it at the visit, and do not stop it on your own.
- **Acid reflux.** Stomach acid creeping up irritates the throat. A typical clue is coughing or throat clearing after meals or at bedtime, sometimes with heartburn.
- **Whooping cough (pertussis).** Rapid-fire coughing spells that end with a high-pitched "whoop" when breathing in, or with vomiting. It spreads easily and is especially dangerous for babies in the home.
- **Pneumonia.** Fever that comes back, pain when taking a deep breath and fatigue that worsens after an initial improvement.
- **Tuberculosis.** Uncommon, but worth keeping in mind when a cough goes past three weeks along with fever, night sweats or unexplained weight loss. The [tuberculosis test](/services/prueba-tuberculosis) is done at the clinic.

## What you can do at home in the meantime

If the cough is slowly improving and there are no warning signs, these steps help:

1. **Drink more water than usual.** It keeps mucus thinner and easier to bring up.
2. **Honey in warm tea or by the spoonful,** for adults and children over one year old. Never for babies under one.
3. **Moist air.** A clean humidifier or steam from a hot shower soothes nighttime irritation.
4. **No smoke.** No cigarettes, no vaping, no charcoal grill indoors. Smoke is what drags bronchitis out the longest.
5. **Sleep with your head slightly raised** if the cough worsens when you lie flat.
6. **Ask before giving cough syrup to young children.** Over-the-counter cough medicines are not recommended for the little ones.

Jot down when you cough most, whether there is mucus and what color, and whether you have had a fever. That information makes the visit far more useful.

## Signs to get checked soon, not next week

- The cough has gone past three weeks
- The fever came back after a few days without it
- You hear wheezing or get winded with light effort
- One side of your chest hurts when you breathe deeply
- Coughing spells end in vomiting or a high-pitched sound on the in-breath
- There are streaks of blood in your mucus
- You have asthma, diabetes or heart disease, or you smoke, and the cough is not letting up
- There is a newborn at home and you cannot stop coughing

## When it is an emergency: call 911

- Trouble breathing at rest or while talking
- Chest pain or pressure that does not go away
- Bluish lips or face
- Confusion, unusual drowsiness or difficulty waking up
- Coughing up a large amount of blood

## What happens at the visit

Through the [respiratory illness](/services/enfermedades-respiratorias) service at Clínica Hispana La Caridad, the clinic's medical team examines your throat, ears and lungs, checks your temperature and blood oxygen, and asks how the cough has evolved since day one. If your symptoms are recent, a rapid flu or COVID test can be run with results in minutes.

From there, the next step is decided: relief for the cough, treatment for an asthma flare, treatment for an infection if one is found, or a referral for an additional test when one is needed. If a medicine for the cough or your airways is part of the plan, you leave the clinic with it in hand and a clear explanation of how and for how many days to take it.

To get the most out of the visit, bring:

- The approximate date your cold started
- What you have already taken for the cough and whether it helped
- A list of all your medicines, including blood pressure pills
- Whether you smoke or vape, and whether anyone at home has the same cough

## Looking ahead to next season

A long cough cannot always be prevented, but the risk of complications can be lowered. This season's flu shot and the Tdap booster, which also protects against whooping cough, are given walk-in through the [vaccines](/services/vacunas) service. Washing hands and not sharing cups at home while someone is coughing help too.
