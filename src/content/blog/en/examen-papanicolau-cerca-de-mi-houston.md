## What is a Pap smear?

The Pap smear (also called a Pap test or Papanicolaou test) is a simple screening that detects changes in the cells of the cervix **before** they turn into cancer. It's the most effective tool for preventing cervical cancer, and when done regularly, this cancer is almost 100% preventable.

Collecting the sample takes less than five minutes: the doctor inserts a speculum, gently collects a small sample of cervical cells with a soft brush, and sends it to the lab. It can feel a little uncomfortable, but it shouldn't hurt.

## How often should I get a Pap smear?

Current medical guidelines recommend:

| Age | Recommended frequency |
|-----|-----------------------|
| 21 to 29 | Pap smear every 3 years |
| 30 to 65 | Pap smear every 3 years, or Pap + HPV test every 5 years |
| Over 65 | May stop if previous results have been normal |

If you've had an abnormal result, have HPV, a weakened immune system, or a history of cervical cancer, your doctor may recommend more frequent screening.

## How to prepare for the test

For the most accurate result:

- Schedule the test when you're **not on your period** (ideally days 10–20 of your cycle).
- Avoid intercourse, douching, vaginal suppositories or creams for **48 hours** beforehand.
- You don't need to fast.
- Bring your ID and, if you have them, results from previous tests.

## What do the results mean?

- **Normal (negative):** no abnormal cells found. Repeat according to your age.
- **ASC-US or LSIL (mild changes):** minor changes, often caused by HPV that the body clears on its own. Usually the test is repeated in 6 to 12 months.
- **HSIL or significant changes:** requires further evaluation (colposcopy). It doesn't mean you have cancer, but it does mean acting soon.

Whatever the result, we explain it clearly and, if a specialist is needed, we refer you with your results ready so you don't lose time.
